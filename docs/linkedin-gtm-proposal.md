# LinkedIn GTM Proposal

**Status:** Working draft for validation

**Scope:** Organic LinkedIn presence and AI-search discoverability for Jennifer Perret, AI CRAFTWORKS, and AgentCraftworks

**North star:** Help Microsoft-oriented organizations understand how to move from AI experimentation toward human-plus-agent ways of working, trust Jennifer's perspective, and take a measurable next step with AICraftworks.

## Executive proposal

Treat LinkedIn as both a professional distribution channel and a discoverable body of evidence about who Jennifer helps, what AICraftworks is focused on, and what AgentCraftworks is being developed to address. Build one connected narrative across three identities, not three competing brands:

| Identity | GTM job | What it should be known for |
|---|---|---|
| **Jennifer Perret** | Founder voice and primary relationship-builder | The founder thesis that AI capability is moving faster than organizational readiness, and the practical work of building human-plus-agent operating models |
| **AI CRAFTWORKS** | Company and services context | Helping organizations become AI-native and understand, build, govern, and scale agentic work through consulting and AI-native product development |
| **AgentCraftworks** | Product vision and technical learning | The working category is an Enterprise Agent Operations Platform: an intended system of record and operating workflow for enterprise agents |

Lead with Jennifer's personal profile and useful, native LinkedIn content. Use the AI CRAFTWORKS Company Page as a complete, consistent reference for the company's three-part portfolio: consulting, AI-native product development, and AgentCraftworks. Introduce AgentCraftworks as a product in development, linking only to evidence that exists and is approved for public use. Keep the founder's perspective distinct from service offers and product claims.

The strategic shift is from optimizing only for reach or website clicks to earning **recognition, trust, and qualified discovery conversations**. LinkedIn should initially support category education, founder-led outreach, customer discovery, and ecosystem relationships—not be treated as a proven SaaS demand-generation engine. Clear language, substantive answers, consistent terminology, and evidence should make the story easier for people—and potentially AI search systems—to understand. AI-search visibility is an intended benefit, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Why this approach

LinkedIn's [2026 article on AI search visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/ai-search/how-b2b-marketers-can-dominate-ai-search-on-linkedin-2026/) says buyers increasingly use generative AI in B2B research and introduces a guide based on LinkedIn's internal testing and findings. The article describes declining non-branded search traffic on LinkedIn's marketing sites and cites external 6sense and Gartner figures. LinkedIn's [guide landing page](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/unlocking-ai-visibility) frames its recommendations around credibility, content structure, and trusted platforms, and reports that LinkedIn is the most-cited domain for professional queries in AI search.

Those are **source-reported claims**, not independently verified results or forecasts for these brands. The public guide landing page does not expose its full playbook. Accordingly, this proposal applies the guide's stated direction without attributing specific tactics or outcomes to LinkedIn that could not be verified. Validate the complete guide and actual account data before making investment or performance commitments.

Treat this as a recruiting hypothesis for discovery, not a validated market segment.

**Roles to test in LinkedIn content and discovery**

- AI, automation, or transformation leaders accountable for AI portfolio visibility, standards, and operating reviews.
- Enterprise architects, agent-platform owners, and builders responsible for moving agent initiatives from experimentation into repeatable operations.
- Security, privacy, risk, compliance, and data-governance stakeholders who need ownership, access boundaries, approvals, and evidence.

**Validation needed:** Which buyer experiences the problem most acutely, who owns it and its budget, what event creates urgency, which existing workaround is used, what evidence would support action, and whether the right initial offer is a readiness assessment, consulting/build engagement, design partnership, or product evaluation.

## Content strategy

### Editorial pillars

1. **From AI experimentation to agentic operations:** Explain how organizations move from isolated pilots toward repeatable, accountable human-plus-agent ways of working.
2. **Operating-model fundamentals:** Explore agent inventory, accountable ownership, business purpose, risk classification, approvals, lifecycle status, and human oversight as governance concepts and discovery questions.
3. **Operational evidence and value:** Discuss what leaders may need to review—usage, quality, exceptions, incidents, costs, and business outcomes—without suggesting those measures are already available in AgentCraftworks.
4. **AI-native products and Microsoft-oriented transformation:** Share practical thinking about AI strategy, workforce enablement, product design, and building in a Microsoft-oriented enterprise context. Describe ecosystem alignment as a focus, not a partnership or completed integration.
5. **Founder learning and category discovery:** Share specific build lessons and ask focused questions that surface how organizations manage agents today, where workflow breaks down, who owns the problem, and what a pilot would need to prove.

### Formats and execution

- Use Jennifer's profile as the main publishing and conversation surface. Start with a sustainable pilot of **one or two useful posts per week**; adjust based on quality, workload, and audience response rather than chasing volume.
- Make the early pilot primarily **category education and customer discovery**. Test the buyer, problem, urgency, current workaround, and first offer before scaling SaaS-oriented promotion or fixing the category language permanently.
- Publish a deeper LinkedIn article or newsletter edition around a recurring buyer question when the material merits it. Use an answer-forward title, a concise summary, clear section headings, definitions, examples, and links to supporting evidence. Do not reformat every short post into long-form content.
- Use a consistent answer-first structure: name the buyer question or problem, give the direct answer in the opening, explain the reasoning in plain language, show a specific example or artifact, and end with one useful takeaway and relevant next step. For long-form content, use descriptive headings that make sections easy to scan. Use searchable terms naturally; avoid keyword stuffing and engagement bait.
- Apply an editorial quality gate before publishing: Is this for a defined professional audience? Does it answer one real question? Is the claim supported and clearly bounded? Can a reader understand the post without the rest of the thread? Is the source or evidence link useful and working?
- Keep a complete AI CRAFTWORKS Company Page with a specific description, website and contact links, consistent logo/branding, and current product context. Repurpose selected founder posts with company-specific framing; do not make the page a duplicate feed.
- Use the product/repository/website as the evidence destination. Each link should substantiate the claim in the post and offer a clear next step, not send every reader to a generic homepage.
- Participate in relevant practitioner discussions with useful answers. Seek authentic collaboration and third-party corroboration where earned; do not solicit scripted endorsements or manufacture social proof.
- Repurpose a strong technical explanation into a site article or documentation page when useful, preserving consistent terminology and linking the LinkedIn discussion to the deeper source.
- Use one primary call to action per post and align it to readiness: invite a relevant conversation or discovery interview first; discuss the Agent Operations Readiness Assessment only after its scope and availability are confirmed; introduce a design partnership or AgentCraftworks evaluation only when there is a qualified fit and an approved offer.

### Example post briefs

- **“You built the agent. Who owns it now?”** Explain why an accountable owner, a clear business purpose, and a lifecycle status are useful operating questions. Invite readers to share how they answer them; do not imply AgentCraftworks already provides an agent registry.
- **“From agent pilot to operating model: what changes?”** Describe the organizational questions around ownership, permissions, governance, deployment discipline, visibility, and measurable value. Ask which is currently the hardest; treat responses as discovery, not representative market research.
- **“What should an agent operating review show?”** Offer a proposed review checklist—portfolio, owners, purpose, risk, lifecycle, incidents, usage, quality, cost, and outcomes—and clearly label future-state items as a framework rather than existing product functionality.
- **“Where should Microsoft fit in an enterprise agent operating model?”** Explore ecosystem-aware design, cross-functional ownership, and evidence without claiming a partnership, certification, or completed product integration.
- **“The hardest part may not be creating the agent.”** Use the founder narrative to explore the gap between AI capability and organizational readiness, then invite qualified leaders to discuss their current approach.

Each brief should answer a real question and stand alone when quoted or excerpted. Include supporting links and a relevant next step—follow, read the technical detail, or join a discovery conversation—rather than stacking calls to action. Claims about customer pain should be framed as hypotheses until customer interviews substantiate them.

## Phased rollout

### Phase 1 — Foundation and baseline

- Confirm the working company/product language with the founder; resolve which consulting and AI-native product-development offers are currently available, AgentCraftworks' actual stage, and which claims are approved.
- Choose the initial discovery sample and interview roles. The documents identify CIO/CTO/CDO/Chief AI Officer, AI CoE or automation leaders, enterprise architects/agent-platform owners, security/privacy/risk/compliance/data-governance leaders, product/business owners, builders, and procurement/legal/vendor management as possible participants; prioritize a manageable subset for learning rather than calling all of them the ICP.
- Audit Jennifer's profile, the AI CRAFTWORKS Company Page, and AgentCraftworks' public product surfaces for clear entity descriptions, consistent terminology, relevant expertise, evidence, working links, and an obvious next step. Record gaps before rewriting.
- Capture current profile/page completeness, follower and audience composition where available, recent reach and engagement, relevant inbound, referral traffic, and existing AI-search mentions.
- Align profile, company, product, and site language; identify gaps in product evidence and conversion destinations. Create a short approved-claims list, mark vision/MVP/roadmap separately, and prepare the reusable answer-first editorial brief before the first post.

### Phase 2 — Message and format pilot

- Run a four-week organic pilot using the editorial pillars and sustainable cadence above.
- Test a small number of message angles: organizational AI readiness, human-plus-agent operating models, agent inventory/ownership/governance, and operational evidence/value. Use Microsoft-oriented examples where appropriate without implying formal Microsoft affiliation.
- Use the editorial quality gate on every post. Track quality signals and conversation themes, not just impressions. Respond to substantive comments and record recurring buyer questions.
- Publish only technical and product claims that have been checked against the implementation and approved for public disclosure.
- Use relevant engagement to invite customer-discovery conversations and test urgency, ownership, workarounds, budget, and pilot success criteria. Where validated and actually offered, use the Agent Operations Readiness Assessment as a next step.

### Phase 3 — Learn and scale deliberately

- Review results against baseline and interview notes. Double down on the topics that attract the intended roles and generate meaningful follow-up.
- Turn repeated questions into durable articles, site resources, or product documentation; strengthen proof before increasing promotional content.
- Test the planned progression from discovery to readiness assessment, consulting/build work, design partnership or product-development engagement, and eventually AgentCraftworks onboarding only where each offer is real and qualified. Track conversion between stages; treat the hypothesis that services accelerate product adoption as unproven until measured.
- Decide whether to expand cadence, establish a LinkedIn newsletter, recruit collaborators, or test paid distribution only after organic message-market evidence and conversion tracking are credible.

These phases describe a proposed sequence, not a commitment to a calendar or a promised growth curve. The pilot duration and starting cadence are working assumptions to confirm against available time.

### Proposed discovery-to-offer path

The operating plan describes a founder-led path from category content and ecosystem outreach to customer discovery, an Agent Operations Readiness Assessment, a qualified assessment/build engagement or design partnership, and eventually AgentCraftworks adoption and expansion. This is the **intended GTM motion in a working plan**, not evidence that every offer is currently packaged or available.

For LinkedIn, use a relevant post to start a conversation; qualify pain, urgency, ownership, budget, workaround, and required proof in discovery; then offer only the next step that is actually ready. Treat polite engagement as interest, not traction. Stronger validation requires concrete follow-through such as a subsequent meeting, access to relevant stakeholders or data, explicit pilot criteria, budget discussion, or design-partner commitment. Confirm the assessment's scope and availability before advertising it.

## Measurement

Establish a baseline before setting numeric targets. Review a lightweight scorecard monthly and make a more substantive pilot decision after four weeks. Measure both LinkedIn visibility and the discovery/offer path; do not equate social engagement with validated demand.

| Goal | Useful measures | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Clear positioning | Profile/page completeness; consistency of descriptions and links; whether target readers can accurately describe the offer in short interviews | Leading indicators; do not treat completeness as visibility |
| Relevant reach | Impressions/reach and follower growth, segmented by target role or audience where LinkedIn provides it | Reach is not buying intent |
| Credible engagement | Substantive comments, saves/shares if available, profile/company visits, repeat interactions from relevant roles | Prioritize relevance and quality over raw likes |
| Demand and learning | Discovery conversations by role; repeated pains and urgency triggers; current workarounds; ownership/budget; objections; concrete follow-through; readiness-assessment interest where available | Separate social engagement from customer evidence; define qualification and log evidence |
| Offer progression | Qualified conversations to assessment, build engagement, design-partner discussion, and product interest/onboarding where those offers are active | Track each transition; test rather than assume that services accelerate software adoption |
| Discoverability | Referral traffic from LinkedIn; appearances in a consistent set of AI-search prompt tests; citations and named entities when present | AI prompt checks are directional snapshots, not a representative ranking or attribution method |

For AI-search prompt tests, keep a small fixed set of realistic buyer questions, record the model/tool and date, capture whether the brand or a useful source appears, and note the cited URLs. Repeat consistently and treat results as exploratory: answers vary by model, location, time, and prompt. Do not claim that a post caused a citation without evidence.

After the baseline, set targets that reflect capacity, current audience size, discovery progress, and the validated commercial path. Avoid importing industry-wide figures or LinkedIn's reported search statistics as this brand's forecast.

## Risks and guardrails

- **Three brands blur together:** Use the brand roles above consistently and give each identity a distinct purpose.
- **Attention without buying relevance:** Optimize for conversations with the intended roles, not viral reach.
- **Unsupported product or compliance claims:** Maintain a simple approved-claims list; separate shipped, experimental, and planned capabilities. A feature is not by itself a certification or guarantee.
- **Founder channel becomes a product broadcast:** Keep useful teaching and honest build lessons at the center; use direct product calls to action selectively.
- **AI-search over-optimization:** Write for human buyers first. Avoid keyword stuffing, fabricated authority, scripted endorsements, or promises of AI citation.
- **Source claims treated as guarantees:** Attribute LinkedIn's and its cited sources' numbers accurately, verify the full guide where possible, and never use them as expected campaign results.
- **Content disclosure and confidentiality:** Review technical examples for credentials, customer information, security-sensitive implementation detail, and employment/confidentiality obligations before publishing.
